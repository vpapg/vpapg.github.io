Code First Girls - Assistant Instructor

Nov 30, 2021

From my personal experience, being a female computer scientist can be challenging. Since my Bachelor's degree studies I had decided I wanted to pursue a PhD related to Artificial Intelligence. I was dreaming of teaching in higher education while also working on research projects in my area. There were many people who supported me and believed in myself. But exactly because I was a 'minority' in this field (there were days in which I was one of a handful if not the only female student in the lecture theatre), there were people who took it for granted that I am at best a mediocre computer scientist who will only teach computing classes at schools after graduation because this is the simplest option. Obviously, there are at least three problems with this way of thinking:

  • What's wrong with teaching at a school? Why does this mean that you are a 'bad' computer scientist? Why a programming teacher cannot be an inspiring person for the students, who can instill knowledge and values?
  • Why teaching (especially at schools) is a "women's" job? Aren't women good enough to work as computer scientists in industry or in academia? And is it less of an achievement for a man to be a teacher at a school because men are supposed to do "better" ("better"??) jobs?
  • Why do so many people believe that when it comes to tech, women are only accidentally there and not because they are as skilled and determined as men?

This is only part of the problem and not the whole struggle. I just wanted to highlight this as an example of how female computer scientists have been treated --which also makes me wonder what the experience of a non-binary student might be.

Fortunately, things changed dramatically during my MA and PhD --this is when I 'forgot' that I am a female computer scientist! I was lucky to study in Sheffield, in one of the most welcoming Computer Science Departments in Europe. The University and the Department work hard on diversity and inclusion, and not only regarding gender-related discrimination.

Such welcoming and non-toxic environments are absolutely necessary for everyone. During my PhD studies, I was glad to learn about Code First Girls (CFG), who offer courses that help women and non-binary people get into tech. My passion for teaching met my experience in being a computer science student, and I ended up being a volunteer co-instructor in three cohorts of their 8-week Introduction to Python course [1][2] from October 2020 to November 2021.

Working with people who value education and provide opportunities to under-represented groups is essential to me and my experience with CFG was positive and rewarding, so I am very glad that this time I will be working as an Assistant Instructor for the upcoming 13-week Nanodegree course, in the Software Development specialisation.

If you are dreaming of pursuing a career in data analysis, software engineering, or full-stack development but you feel that you lack the necessary background, I strongly recommend applying to this course, as it is a great opportunity to expand your knowledge and your skills and transform your career. The applications for the spring cohort are closing on Friday 10th December.

See you there!
